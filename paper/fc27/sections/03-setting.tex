\section{Setting and Data}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{USDC and the SVB shock}
USDC is a fiat-backed stablecoin issued by Circle, redeemable one-for-one against
reserves. Reserves are held in short-dated U.S. Treasuries and bank cash, so a bank
failure impairs backing through the cash leg. The peg depends on full reserve backing and on an
arbitrage loop: when the secondary price falls below par, an authorized participant can
buy cheap tokens and redeem them at \$1.

On March~10, 2023, Silicon Valley Bank failed. Late that evening, Circle disclosed on its
official X account that ``\$3.3 billion of the ${\sim}$\$40 billion of USDC reserves remain
at SVB''~\cite{circle2023tweet}. Circle restated that figure as ``about $8\%$'' of total
reserves in its March~12 press release~\cite{circle2023pressrelease}, and contemporaneous
reporting corroborates it~\cite{capoot2023}. We take $\phi\approx 0.08$, Circle's own
rounded disclosure. The raw ratio $3.3/40.0=8.25\%$ is a different reading of the same
disclosure, and Section~\ref{sec:ident} shows the result is insensitive to the exact
denominator. The secondary-market price fell below par over March~10--11, reaching a
trough of \$0.877. On March~12 at 22:15~UTC, the FDIC, the Federal Reserve, and the
Treasury issued a joint statement guaranteeing SVB depositors in full~\cite{fedtreasfdic2023pressrelease}.
USDC restored its peg over the following days. The disclosed fraction $\phi\approx 0.08$
sets the worst-case floor, and the full re-peg reveals ex post that no
permanent loss occurred.

\subsection{DAI and USDT}
DAI is a decentralized stablecoin issued by the MakerDAO protocol. By 2023 DAI's collateral
pool was linked to USDC through its peg-stability module (PSM). The PSM is a contract
that lets anyone swap USDC for DAI and DAI for USDC one-for-one, up to a debt ceiling. Press
reporting says that ceiling was reached on 11~March 2023~\cite{theblock2023psm}. A USDC discount can therefore be
arbitraged into DAI only while the outstanding balance is below the ceiling. DAI serves
as the \emph{contagion} coin in our placebo. It is exposed to the shock through a collateral
and arbitrage channel (Appendix~\ref{app:dai}). DAI's on-chain USDC backing at the run-date
block is unavailable (Appendix~\ref{app:dai}), so the exposure below rests on cited
reporting.

Tether (USDT), the largest fiat-backed stablecoin, had no disclosed exposure to SVB and
serves as the \emph{unexposed} coin. During the run it drew flight-to-safety demand and
traded at a premium above par. We exploit that upward divergence as an identifying placebo
(Section~\ref{sec:placebo}).

\subsection{Data and the frozen snapshot}
All analysis runs offline on a frozen snapshot over the window 2023-03-08 to 2023-03-17
(UTC). No proprietary data enters the analysis.

Two timestamp conventions appear in the frozen series. Kraken and Coinbase label each
hourly bar by its start, so 07:00~UTC means the bar covering 07:00 to 08:00. The aggregator
composite instead carries the aggregator's own observation timestamps, which fall a minute
or two off the hour, so a composite reading appears as 06{:}59 or 07{:}57. Every UTC time in
this paper is reported as the source recorded it, with no rounding to a common grid, so that
every printed time matches a frozen value.

The headline USD price series is an hourly composite from the public aggregator DeFiLlama.
The frozen record does not enumerate its constituent venues, so we describe it as an
aggregator composite. We do not treat it as an independent venue. Kraken's fiat-settled
USDC/USD pair cross-checks the \$0.877 trough, with 240 complete hourly rows over 8 to
17~March, a span that contains the 216-hour analysis window. Binance \texttt{USDCUSDT} is a
second cross-check. The frozen Binance series starts only at 14:00~UTC on March~11, after
the trough, so it corroborates depth over the later hours. It cannot corroborate the trough.
Because the pair is USDT-denominated, we convert it to USD by multiplying the raw price by
the contemporaneous USDT-USD price, so the converted price embeds USDT's own premium. We do
not assume USDT traded at \$1. We measure the USDT premium separately, on Coinbase
\texttt{USDT-USD}, the second fiat-settled cross-check alongside Kraken.

To measure who redeemed, we reconstruct primary-market redemptions from on-chain burn
events, FIFO-matching (first-in-first-out) each burn to the oldest unconsumed deposit that
reached Circle's minter. Appendix~\ref{app:hhi} reports the coverage this attribution achieves.
The freeze covers the attribution's \emph{output}, not the per-event log it was derived
from (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). For the DAI decomposition we read
an archival \texttt{eth\_call} read of on-chain DAI supply at the run-date block
(Appendix~\ref{app:dai}).
